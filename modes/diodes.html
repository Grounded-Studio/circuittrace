The one-way mode

Diodes: lines with a direction

Arrow cells turn a flow puzzle into a question of direction. Where you enter is up to you, where you leave is not.

How it works

A diode is a cell with an arrow on it. A line may enter from any side, but it has to leave in the direction the arrow points.

That single rule changes how you read a board. An arrow next to a wall, or pointing at another colour, tells you a lot before you draw anything. The first sectors use only a few arrows per level, and the last sector has around six.

From sector 5 every cell has to be filled to solve the level, so no arrow can simply be avoided.

  • ✓ 100 levels in 10 sectors
  • ✓ Boards from 6×6 to 10×10
  • ✓ 4 to 9 colour pairs
  • ✓ One-way arrows on every board

Tips for Diodes

  • Look at where each arrow points first. The cell it points to is almost a guaranteed part of that line.
  • Remember that a line can come into a diode from any side. Only the way out is fixed.
  • An arrow pointing at a dot often means that colour goes through it, right at the end of the line.
